ADR-006 2025-11-10 · ACCEPTED

Kill switch: mechanical vs MOSFET

Context

Safety - how to physically cut thruster power on a signal from the safety diver / referee.

Options considered
  • Mechanical kill switch (Talamex 300 A, IP67)
  • Active MOSFET controlled by software
Decision

Mechanical Talamex 300 A kill switch

Why

Verifiable, fail-safe disconnection with no dependency on software state. An active MOSFET would introduce a software dependency for a safety-critical function - a MOSFET gate-circuit fault could prevent disconnect.

The kill switch is one of the strictest RoboSub requirements: the vehicle must de-energise thrusters within 1 second of activation, but compute must remain online (re-arm without reboot).

Architecture

Talamex Mini Battery Switch (300 A, IP67) mounted externally on the frame, visible to the safety diver. Activation:

  1. Disconnects the main battery from all 8 ESCs and payload servos on the main bus
  2. The compute stack (Jetson, Pixhawk, RPi) remains powered (regulators upstream of the kill switch)
  3. RPi nodes on the aux battery - unaffected

Consequences

  • Software keeps running on kill - the mission planner sees the “killed” state and reacts accordingly
  • Re-arm after a kill does not spin the thrusters automatically - requires an explicit command
  • Zero software dependency for the disconnect itself
