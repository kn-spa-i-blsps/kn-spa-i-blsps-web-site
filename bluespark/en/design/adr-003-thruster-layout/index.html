ADR-003 2025-09-15 · ACCEPTED

Thruster layout: 6 vs 8 in vector configuration

Context

Thruster count and layout for the first BlueSpark vehicle - critical for maneuverability, cost, and torque-allocator scope.

Options considered
  • 6 thrusters (4 horizontal + 2 vertical) - classic AUV layout
  • 8 thrusters in BlueROV2 Heavy vector configuration (4H 45° + 4V) - full 6DOF
  • 5 thrusters (3 horizontal + 2 vertical) - minimalist
Decision

8 thrusters in the BlueROV2 Heavy vector configuration: 4 horizontal (45° vector arms) + 4 vertical.

Why

Full independent control across 6 degrees of freedom (Surge, Sway, Heave, Roll, Pitch, Yaw) with directional redundancy. The Vectored 6DOF frame is natively supported by ArduSub on Pixhawk - torque allocation lives in firmware, zero QP-solver work on our software side.

Motivation

Thruster layout is the hardest decision to reverse in the mechanical project - changing it after the frame is built means redesigning mounts, cable runs, and the control model. We make this call early and stick with it for the season.

Options considered

Option A: 6 thrusters (4H + 2V)

Classic layout - 4 thrusters on the horizontal plane (X or + arrangement) + 2 dedicated to vertical motion.

Pros:

  • Lower hardware cost (4 fewer thrusters + ESCs + wiring).
  • Simpler torque allocation.
  • Lower nominal power draw.

Cons:

  • No independent pitch and roll control - compensation only through trim.
  • Less stable hover in water current.
  • Less common in modern RoboSub-class AUVs.

Option B: 8 thrusters in vector configuration (4H 45° + 4V) — SELECTED

4 horizontal thrusters in a vector layout (45° angle) + 4 vertical at the four corners. BlueROV2 Heavy configuration, full 6DOF.

Pros:

  • Full independent control across 6 degrees of freedom.
  • Redundancy - the vehicle stays maneuverable if one thruster fails.
  • Vectored 6DOF frame natively supported by ArduSub - allocator ships in firmware, no in-house QP implementation needed.
  • Industry standard in modern RoboSub-class AUVs.

Cons:

  • Higher hardware and wiring cost (8× TD1.2 ESC ~1.2 kgf each).
  • Higher nominal power draw.

Option C: 5 thrusters (3H + 2V)

Cons:

  • No power reserve, no redundancy.
  • Insufficient control for RoboSub tasks needing precision positioning (Bins, Torpedoes).

Why 8 in vector layout

  1. Full 6DOF - without it, several RoboSub tasks (precise marker positioning, hovering in current, torpedo maneuvers) become very hard.
  2. Redundancy - a single thruster failure mid-run does not end the run.
  3. ArduSub native - Vectored 6DOF frame with off-the-shelf allocator in firmware. Zero implementation cost on our software.
  4. Industry standard - configuration validated through many design iterations.

Consequences

  • The frame accommodates 8 thruster mounting points (symmetric BlueROV2 Heavy frame).
  • Electronics - 2× Holybro PDB-300A → 8 ESC channels, total draw factored into the power chain (2× ANL 300 A fuses).
  • Software - torque allocation handled by ArduSub (firmware), not by us. ROS 2 sends 6DOF setpoints, ArduSub distributes them across 8 PWM channels.
  • Validation - per-thruster test (January 2026) + 6DOF validation in the pool (planned 2026-05-20).
